Consulted bridges · project outline

Bridge Under Conformity

When an AI agent sees other agents' answers, does its own answer still come from its own reasoning, or does it take the group's answer? We test this on two-hop questions by removing the hidden middle step inside the model and watching what changes.

The goal in one paragraph

Take “What letter does the name of the country where the Colosseum is located start with?”. To answer “I”, the model has to think of Italy, a word that is never written: the bridge. Alone, removing Italy's direction inside the model destroys the answer, so the answer depends on the model's own path through Italy. Now show the same question with three other agents answering first. If they say I, does the model still go through Italy, or does it just repeat them? If they say S and the model gives in, is Italy still pulling it toward “I”? The paper we build on (Silent Dissent) says the bridge is still present when agents give in. We test whether it is still used.

Why these questions

The test decides the question type, not the other way round

We did not set out to study letter questions. We set out to test whether an agent's answer in a group still comes from its own reasoning. That test only works on questions with certain properties, and the question format follows from those properties.

What a question needs for the test to work

  • 1Alone, the answer depends on the bridge. Removing the bridge's direction must destroy the answer when the model answers by itself. Otherwise there is no “own reasoning” to look for in the group.
  • 2The bridge and the answer are different things inside the model. If the answer is the bridge or close to it in meaning (York University → Toronto → Canada), Toronto's direction already contains part of “Canada”. Removing the bridge then also removes part of the answer, and we can't tell “the model used the bridge” from “we deleted the answer”. The answer has to be an arbitrary property of the bridge, one that shares nothing with its meaning.
  • 3The input can't give the answer without the bridge. There must be no shortcut from the question's words straight to the answer, or the model could answer without its own middle step.
  • 4The peers dispute the answer, not the bridge. The peers' wrong answer must be a plausible alternative of the same kind, so the model can either copy it or work out its own. The bridge then stays untouched as a probe of the model's own path.
  • 5One token per answer. So the probabilities of the right answer and the peers' answer can be compared directly, and the peers' answer is a single, matched alternative.

What fits

The first letter of a named place or thing meets all five. “What letter does the name of the country where the Colosseum is located start with?” needs Italy (1); a letter shares nothing with a country's meaning (2); nothing in “Colosseum” leads to “I” except through Italy's spelling (3); the peers can say another letter (4); the answer is one token (5).

Other arbitrary properties would fit too, such as a count (legs of the animals that spin webs) or a number fact about a place. Letter questions are simply the type that passes the solo test in large enough numbers on both models.

What doesn't

Answers that are the bridge's meaning or category fail point 2: the country of a city (York University → Canada), an element's symbol (sodium → Na), a month after a holiday. There the bridge and the answer overlap inside the model, and removing one removes part of the other.

Answers the input already gives fail point 3: “In which month do children go trick-or-treating?” reaches October without needing Halloween.

So the narrow scope is a consequence of testability. The method gets clean results on questions whose answer is an arbitrary property of the bridge, and can't separate bridge from answer on the rest. We say so, and claim nothing about the other types.

What we claim, and what we don't

The claim

On letter-type two-hop questions where Qwen's answer alone depends on the bridge, we measure whether removing that bridge still changes the answer when the model is in a group that agrees or disagrees with it, and we compare that with whether the bridge is still readable (the paper's measure).

Not claimed: that the model reasons “through Italy” as a step; that the direction is Italy rather than a related signal; anything about other question types (the paper's own city-to-country facts are out of scope).

Stage 1 · single questions

Which two-hop questions

All questions whose answer is a letter and where the existing solo scorecard found the bridge needed early (beats 12 everyday words by 0.5) and acting before the answer forms. Questions built as controls to fail are left out. No new selection run: the lists come from the saved solo results.

Qwen3.6-27B · 25 questions (main model)

Letter of the country of a landmark (Colosseum, Eiffel Tower, Mount Fuji, Kremlin, Machu Picchu, Petronas Towers, Leaning Tower of Pisa, pyramids, Sagrada Família, Sydney Opera House, Angkor Wat); of a person's birth city (Bill Gates, Édith Piaf, Mozart, Muhammad Ali, Beyoncé, Chris Hemsworth, Jimi Hendrix, Michelle Obama); of a university's city (York, Alberta, Berklee, Fudan, Pompeu Fabra); and of the animals that spin webs.

Qwen3.5-4B · 24 questions (replication)

The same families, with the questions this smaller model can answer and pass: 14 landmark countries, 6 birth cities, 4 university cities.

The six calibration questions

Colosseum, Gates, Piaf, Mozart, spider and York were used in the earlier pilot, and the reading thresholds below were set on their numbers. They are run with everything else, but reported separately: the headline comes from the other questions only.

Stage 2 · groups

The prompts

One user message in the model's chat template. The peers' lines come after the question. The instruction is neutral: it does not tell the model to take the peers into account.

Alone 
What letter does the name of the country where
the Colosseum is located start with? Answer with
one letter.

The model says I.

Group (1, 2 or 3 peers) 
Several AI agents are answering the same question.

Question: What letter does the name of the country
where the Colosseum is located start with?
Agent 2: F
Agent 3: F
Agent 4: F

You are Agent 1. Give your own answer. Answer with
one letter.

Agree: the peers say I. Disagree: they all say the same wrong letter, here F. For the calibration questions the wrong letter is the pilot's; for the others it is the first letter of a same-kind word (Germany → G for the Eiffel Tower).

Copy-only control

The calibration questions with a made-up subject (“the country where the Varrenth Arch is located”) and three peers giving a letter. The model has no bridge of its own, so removing the original bridge must do nothing. This shows the measure reads zero when copying is the only option.

Per model: 7 prompts per question (alone, agree × 3, disagree × 3) plus 6 copy-only prompts.

What we hope to find

Expectations, and what each outcome means

From the six-question pilot. Each one can come out either way.

Peers disagree, model holds

The bridge is what holds it

Expect: bridge still pulls. If not: the model resists for some other reason than its own answer.

Peers disagree, partial give-in

Silent dissent, shown causally

Expect: bridge still pulls: the model's own answer is still pushing back while it gives in. If not: the paper's “silent dissent” isn't doing work even here.

Peers disagree, full give-in

The pull fades

Expect: mixed or not needed. If the bridge still pulls: strong support for the paper's reading.

Peers agree

The model stops using its own path

Expect: bridge not needed in most questions, even though the answer is right. Meaning: agreement short-circuits the model's own reasoning.

Presence × use

Present but unused

Expect: in agree prompts and full give-ins, the bridge is still readable but no longer needed. Meaning: there, the paper's presence measure would overstate the model's own reasoning.

More peers

A slide, not a switch

Expect: P(right) and the bridge's pull fall with each added peer. Meaning: how fast group pressure overrides the model's own answer.

Stated up front

Limits

  • 1One question type (letters), two Qwen models, one wording of the group prompt.
  • 2We show whether the bridge's direction still feeds the answer, not what that direction is.
  • 3The reading thresholds were set on the six calibration questions; they stay fixed for the rest.
  • 4Full give-ins sit at the edge of what can be measured (P(right) near 0), which is why the balance LO is used.
