Consulted bridges · conformity follow-up

Copy or Backup Route

When peers give the right letter, removing the bridge often changes nothing. This follow-up asks why. Either the answer now comes only from copying the peers, and the bridge is present but not used. Or the model still works the answer out through the bridge, and copying covers for it when the bridge is removed: a backup route.

New findings only. The main run is in Bridge Under Conformity Results, the plan in the project outline. Same questions, prompts, onsets and labels as the main run; counts below are the new questions only, and the six calibration questions are reported apart.

What we found

  1. On the 27B, the bridge is still used when removing it does nothing. In 17 of 20 agree prompts where the bridge was “not needed”, swapping it for another city or country flips the answer, against up to three peers giving the right letter. 69% of swaps follow, against 80% when the question is asked alone. That is a backup route, not an unused bridge.
  2. With the deferential wording the 27B is split: swaps flip the answer in 17 of 31 “not needed” prompts (43% of swaps).
  3. The 4B with the neutral wording looks like the 27B: 13 of 14 “not needed” prompts follow at least one swap (48% of swaps).
  4. The 4B with the deferential wording copies. Swaps flip the answer in 4 of 43 “not needed” prompts (6 of 172 swaps), and move it only slightly more than an everyday word does. Here the bridge really is present but not used.
  5. It is the letter, not the group, that switches the bridge off. When the three peers write “(answer submitted)” instead of a letter, the bridge is needed again almost as much as alone (27B: 0.96 against 0.98 alone and 0.11 with three agreeing letters).
  6. Give-ins where the bridge wasn't needed are copying. No swap flips any of them (27B deferential: 0 of 16; 4B deferential: 0 of 56).
  7. This revises one claim in the main report. “Present but unused” (finding 3 there) holds for the 4B under the deferential wording. On the 27B, most “not needed” prompts still use the bridge alongside copying: removal alone misses use when a second route can cover.

What changed from the plan

The plan was to block the copy route: remove the peers' letter direction at the peers' tokens, then remove both that and the bridge. On the give-ins, where blocking copying should bring the right answer back, it didn't work.

Median change in the balance (log P(peers') − log P(right)); negative is toward the right letter. On the 27B it moves the balance no more than removing an everyday word at the same tokens; on the 4B it pushes the model further toward the peers. The letter's identity at those tokens isn't carried by that one direction, so this edit can't remove the copy route.

What we did instead

Stopped it after 111 prompts (about 15 minutes) and ran the project's swap test, with the rules fixed before any swap results (docs/swap_test_rules.md): Neuronpedia's move of the bridge onto another word of its kind, on the question's own words, over all layers before the prompt's answer onset. Same question, same budget. The partial copy-block results are kept in results/conformity_routes/.

How the swap tells them apart

Removing a part that has a backup does nothing, so removal can't tell a backup route from an unused bridge. Changing the part's value can. If the answer still reads the bridge, a bridge that now says Munich conflicts with peers saying S, and the answer should move toward M. If nothing reads the bridge, the swap does nothing.

Checks

  • ✓The swap is specific to the bridge. Moving the bridge onto an everyday word (table, river, garden…) made that word's letter the answer in 10 of 1,396 readable cases. Moving a word the model isn't using onto the first target flipped the answer in 2 of 938. 459 of 469 prompt settings count under the fixed rules (an everyday word readable, none gets through).
  • ✓Same prompts as the main run. Baseline P(right) is within 0.02 of the main run in 426 of 469 swap prompts; the largest difference is 0.11 (Piaf, one agreeing peer, a calibration question). The swap run scores a different set of letters in one batch, which shifts the numerics slightly.
  • ✓No failed prompts in the swap run on either model.

Limits

  • 1Removal and swap use different settings. Removal (the “need” labels) edits the question to the end of the prompt in 6-layer windows; the swap edits the question's own words over every layer before the onset, as its rules fix. Within the swap run, removing the bridge at the swap's settings leaves the right answer in nearly every agree prompt and in most alone prompts (95% on the 27B, 75% on the 4B), so the swap is not leaning on a removal that already kills the answer.
  • 2A swap is a strong edit. The controls show it is specific to the bridge's content, but “follows” is the top letter among the letters scored, not a free reply.
  • 3Letter questions, two models, two wordings. The split between the 27B and the 4B under the deferential wording may be about size, wording, or both.
  • 4The copy route itself is still not removed in place. Hiding the letter changes the input, so the model can recompute. The swap avoids that, but no edit here deletes copying alone.
