# Bridge scorecard: consulted bridge or answer echo?

*Qwen3.5-4B, J-lens `qwen-n1000`. Script: `src/consulted_bridges/legacy/bridge_card.py`; sentences in `src/consulted_bridges/legacy/bridge_specs.py`; raw numbers in `results/bridge_card_qwen35_4b/cards.csv` (git-ignored).*

## The setup

**The question:** a one-word question whose middle step is never written.
- "How many legs do the animals that spin webs have?" The middle step is **spider**; the answer is Eight.

**Bridge:** that hidden middle step. It usually ranks near the top of the J-lens word list somewhere in the prompt. The scorecard asks whether the model actually **uses** it to choose the answer.

**Edits:** we change the model's internal state along the J-lens direction of a word, from the question's first word on, 6 layers at a time.
- **Remove:** take the word's direction out.
- **Remove the unique part:** take out only the part of the word's direction that it doesn't share with the answer's direction. ("Salzburg minus Austria".)
- **Swap:** exchange the bridge's direction with another word's (spider ↔ bee).

**Drop:** how much the model's probability of the right answer falls. 1 = gone, 0 = unchanged. We report the best 6-layer window.

## Names for the two qualities

- **Consulted bridge** (spider):
  - the model works the middle step out after reading the question, then reads it to choose the answer;
  - its own part, not shared with the answer, is what matters;
  - other words of its kind don't do the same job.
- **Answer echo** (Salzburg):
  - removing it breaks the answer, but only because its direction largely *is* the answer (or its whole category: "Austrian place");
  - its own part does little, no more than another word of its kind;
  - it acts on the question's own words, where it's stored with the subject (Mozart), not after them.
- **Bystander:** it shows up in J-space, but removing it doesn't change the answer.

## The criteria

| | Criterion | What it measures | Pass | Spider | Salzburg |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **N** | Necessity | drop from removing the bridge | ≥ 0.8 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| **S** | Specificity | N minus the biggest drop from removing an equally strong, unrelated word (picked automatically) | ≥ 0.5 | 0.86 | 0.39 |
| **O** | Answer overlap | how much the bridge's direction is the answer's (cosine) | reported only | 0.22 | 0.55 |
| **U** | Unique need | drop from removing only the part of the bridge not shared with the answer | ≥ 0.5 | **0.99** | 0.58 |
| **D** | Distinctness | U minus the biggest U of a same-kind word (another animal; another Austrian city) | ≥ 0.3 | **0.40** | 0.23 |
| **T** | After reading | drop when removing it only *after* the question, minus drop when removing it only *on* the question's words | ≥ 0 | **+0.82** | −0.06 |
| **K** | Same-answer swap | share of swaps to a word with the *same* answer that keep it (octopus; Innsbruck) | ≥ 0.5 | 0.50 | **0.00** |
| **F** | Follows swaps | share of swaps to a word with a different answer that move the answer away (reported only) | — | 0.75 | 0.88 |

**Verdict rules:**
- **Consulted bridge:** passes N, S, U, D, K and T.
- **Answer echo:** passes N but fails U, D or K.
- **Bystander:** N < 0.5.
- **Unclear:** anything else.

The thresholds were set by looking at spider and Salzburg only, so they're a first guess: two examples can't calibrate them. Fix them before scoring new sentences, then don't move them.

## Results on six sentences

| Sentence | Bridge → answer | N | S | O | U | D | T | K | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| spider legs | spider → Eight | 1.00 | 0.86 | 0.22 | 0.99 | 0.40 | +0.82 | 0.50 | **Consulted bridge** (6/6) |
| Mozart, city wording | Salzburg → Austria | 1.00 | 0.39 | 0.55 | 0.58 | 0.23 | −0.06 | 0.00 | **Answer echo** (2/6) |
| Mozart, direct ("from?") | Salzburg → Austria | 1.00 | 0.74 | 0.55 | 0.24 | 0.08 | −0.20 | 0.75 | **Answer echo** (3/6) |
| apple colour | apple → Red | 0.60 | 0.46 | 0.35 | 0.02 | 0.02 | +0.33 | 1.00 | Unclear (2/6) |
| elephant legs | elephant → Four | 0.00 | — | 0.30 | 0.01 | — | 0.00 | 1.00 | **Bystander** |
| Eiffel capital | France → Paris | 0.00 | — | 0.64 | 0.00 | — | 0.00 | — | **Bystander** |

**What these show:**
- **Salzburg, and the "Salzburg minus Austria" test:**
  - Removing all of Salzburg takes P(Austria) from 0.48 to 0.00.
  - Removing only its unique part brings it to 0.23. That's no better than "Vienna minus Austria" (0.34).
  - In the direct wording it's 0.75 → 0.57 vs Vienna's 0.63.
  - So most of Salzburg's effect was its shared "Austria" part. Spider minus "eight" still takes P(8) from 0.88 to 0.01: the two share only 3% of their direction.
- **Apple:** removing it hurts "Red" (0.60), but its unique part does nothing (0.02). It behaves like an echo even though N is below the echo threshold. Under the current rules it is "unclear".
- **Elephant:** a bystander, although it ranks first in J-space. Without it the model still says "Four", probably because that's a typical animal's count anyway. So the scorecard can't show it's needed. The question type itself can't test this bridge.
- **Eiffel:** a bystander too, as expected. "Eiffel Tower" leads to Paris directly, so the model doesn't need France.

## What this means for Silent Dissent

- **Ranking first in J-space is not enough:** all six bridges ranked first or near it, yet only one is a consulted bridge.
- **The paper's main fact type is the Salzburg case:** the bridge is a birth city, and the answer is its country.
- **A readout that counts the bridge as present can't tell consulted from echo from bystander.** That is the gap in the "silent dissent" claim.

## How to score a new sentence

1. Add an entry to `src/consulted_bridges/legacy/bridge_specs.py`:
   - the question;
   - the bridge;
   - the answers and their written forms;
   - the answer's own word(s) to project out;
   - 2–3 same-kind words;
   - swaps with their expected answers, including at least one with the *same* answer.
2. On the pod: `uv run python scripts/bridge_card.py --spec <name>`. It takes about 2 minutes per sentence.

## Caveats

- **Small sample:** one model, one wording per fact (two for Mozart), six sentences.
- **Our own edit definitions:** remove, unique-part removal and swap are written out in `src/consulted_bridges/interventions.py`.
- **Imperfect automatic controls:** they're words the J-lens shows that overlap less than 0.4 with the bridge and the answer. Some are translations of the answer's topic (Russian or German for "capital"). D uses the hand-picked same-kind words instead.
- **When the model's fallback answer is the correct one** (elephant → "Four"), removal can't show need. Such sentences need a question where the default answer differs.
- **K has few swaps** (2 to 3 per sentence), so it's coarse.

## Results

| Verdict | Sentences |
|---|---|
| **Consulted bridge** | **none** |
| Answer echo | Mozart; CN Tower → Ottawa; Christ the Redeemer → Brasília |
| Unclear | Che Guevara (N = 0.66 but U = 0.13) |
| Bystander | Colosseum, Kremlin, Taj Mahal, Mishima, Djokovic, Federer, Ronaldo, Stalin, Bieber, Tsinghua, Fordham, Harvard, McGill |
| Not known (wrong baseline answer) | Sydney Opera House → says **Sydney**; Keanu Reeves → says **Canada**; Larry Page → Berkeley; Natalie Portman → New York |

- **The "trap" questions show the shortcut directly.** Where the shortcut gives a wrong answer (Sydney Opera House, Keanu Reeves), the model gives that wrong answer. It doesn't route through the bridge.
- **Most of the paper's known birth-city facts have the capital as the bridge:** 76% of the 545 known. Tokyo alone appears 91 times. That's group B1, all bystanders here.
- **Every bridge ranked at or near the top of the J-lens list.** Ranks were 0–3 except Djokovic (17), Stalin (15) and Fordham (44).

## What the criteria did

- **N decided 13 of the 17 scored sentences on its own** (all the bystanders).
- **U and D decided the 3 echoes.** S disagreed once: Christ the Redeemer has S = 0.89 but U = 0.01.
- **K, F and T carry no information when N ≈ 0:** nothing changes, so K is trivially 1. They should only be scored when N ≥ 0.5.

## Caveat that matters most

**"Bystander" means removing the J-lens direction of the bridge does nothing.** That holds if the J-lens direction *is* how the model represents the bridge. If the model holds "Basel" in a form the J-lens direction misses, removing the direction can't show need. Spider shows the method *can* detect need, but not that it always does. Round 3 adds a check for this.
